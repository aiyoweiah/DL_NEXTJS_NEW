## MODULE A — new guide section: **§ The Research Base** (practical reference for writers)

> **Purpose in the guide:** give writers dated, quotable, citation-backed facts so any conversion or GEO surface can carry evidence, not adjectives. Per the GEO method, citing sources / statistics / quotations is the highest-leverage way to be cited by LLMs. **Deploy on:** `/methodology` (a "Why this works" block), `llms-full.txt`, one `/faq` entry, `schema` `citation` nodes.

### A1 · The five claims DODO is licensed to make (each tied to evidence)

| # | Permitted claim (writer-ready) | Backed by | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **"Teaching the Latin and Greek roots beneath English is one of the most-researched strategies in education."** | RFWP editorial framing + the meta-analyses below | Say "most-researched," not "proven best" |
| 2 | **"About 60% of English comes from Latin and Greek — over 90% of the words in science. Learn the roots, and you hold a key to the academic vocabulary of every subject."** | Henry (1997), cited widely incl. NASET | Keep the two numbers paired with "roots," not freestanding |
| 3 | **"In a study of 493 students, root-based vocabulary teaching outperformed memorization — for gifted and typically-developing students alike."** | Gallagher (2017), *Roeper Review* | Never "for struggling/ELL students" — pair gifted + typical |
| 4 | **"Across dozens of controlled studies, teaching the structure of words produces measurable gains in vocabulary, decoding, and spelling."** | Goodwin & Ahn (2010, 2013); Bowers, Kirby & Deacon (2010) | Cite d-values only on `/methodology`, not in hero copy |
| 5 | **"Children grow most on rich, challenging text — not simplified readers. That is why we read real, unabridged classics, set deliberately above the comfortable level."** | Shanahan; Keys to Literacy (challenging-text research) | This validates the 80L–120L-above Read-step choice |

### A2 · The citations (drop-in reference list for `/methodology` + `llms-full.txt`)

- **Gallagher, S. A. (2017).** "Exploring the Efficacy of *The Word Within the Word* for Gifted and Typically Developing Students." *Roeper Review*, 39(2). — 493 middle-school students, 10 teachers; root-based vocabulary beat traditional instruction (moderate effect sizes on sentence completion, Gr 6–7; **moderate-to-large on recall at every grade level**), for **both gifted and typically-developing** students.
- **Goodwin, A. P., & Ahn, S. (2010).** "A meta-analysis of morphological interventions." *Annals of Dyslexia*, 60. — 17 studies; morphological (root/stem) instruction improved literacy achievement overall (**d = 0.33**), including **vocabulary d = 0.40** and morphological awareness d = 0.40.
- **Goodwin, A. P., & Ahn, S. (2013).** "A Meta-Analysis of Morphological Interventions in English." *Scientific Studies of Reading*, 17. — 30 studies; overall **d = 0.32**, with **decoding d = 0.59, vocabulary d = 0.34, spelling d = 0.30**.
- **Bowers, P. N., Kirby, J. R., & Deacon, S. H. (2010).** "The Effects of Morphological Instruction on Literacy Skills: A Systematic Review." *Review of Educational Research*, 80. — 22 studies; benefits all learners, **works best woven into other literacy instruction** (validates DODO's integrated model over isolated drills).
- **Henry (1997)** — ~60% of words in English text are Latin/Greek in origin; >90% in science and technology.
- **VanTassel-Baska, J. (2003).** "Differentiating the Language Arts for High-Ability Learners." Council for Exceptional Children (ERIC E640).
- **Supporting transfer evidence:** Pampush & Petto (2011, *Anatomical Sciences Education*) — root knowledge and anatomy course performance; McIntosh (1994, *Mathematics Teacher*) — word roots in geometry; Rasinski, Padak, Newton & Newton (2011, *The Reading Teacher*).
- **L2 evidence (use only reframed as acceleration):** Bellomo (1999, *TESL-EJ*); Diaz (2010).

### A3 · Ready-to-use reference sentences (original DODO voice, no MCT name, no remediation register)

- "English is built from Latin and Greek — about sixty percent of it, and over ninety percent of the language of science. We teach the roots beneath the words, so a term your child has never seen becomes one they can take apart and solve."
- "We don't ask children to memorize word lists. We teach the structure of the language — the research shows this is what makes vocabulary stick, and transfer, across every subject."
- "The research is consistent: children grow most on rich, complex text, not simplified readers. So we read real, unabridged classics — set a step above the comfortable level, on purpose."
- "Measured, not asserted: in a study of 493 students, teaching word roots outperformed memorization for advanced and typical learners alike."

### A4 · The one hard rule (anti-dictionary tie-in)

The evidence is strongest in ELL / struggling-reader / dyslexia populations. **Never deploy it that way.** DODO's frame is **acceleration into mastery**, not catching up. Forbidden: "closes the ELL gap," "for struggling readers," "remediation." Permitted: "the method behind our vocabulary track is one of the most-researched strategies in education."

## MODULE B — refreshed **§06**: The LCS System (Five Strands nested)

> Replaces the current §06 "LCS Teaching System" 3-row table + the loose MCT book-list. LCS stays the **named umbrella methodology**; the five content strands sit **under the fitting branch.** Speaking is DODO's own — call it out.

### The LCS System — DODO's live delivery architecture

**The LCS System** (语言循环体系) is the curriculum framework that organizes every cycle. Three branches; the content strands of a complete language education nest inside them.

| Branch | What it is | Content strands nested here |
|---|---|---|
| **L · Literacy** — 文学精读 | The reading treasury — where language goes in. | **Literature** (whole, unabridged classics, read for craft) · **Vocabulary** (the Latin/Greek roots of English — structure, not memorization) · **Poetics** (the ear for how great writing is built) |
| **C · Composition** — 系统写作训练 | The construction engine — where language comes out. | **Grammar** (the architecture of the sentence, taught first and fast, then applied) · **Writing** (sentence → paragraph → essay → academic composition) |
| **S · Speaking** — 表达 | DODO's own strand. | Oral defense · Socratic dialogue · perspective-taking. **No equivalent in a curriculum of books** — this is what a live Navigator adds, and it is our clearest differentiator. |

**Naming rules (unchanged from D1/D19):** "The LCS System" (EN) / "语言循环体系" (ZH). **The Loop** (Read → Think → Speak → Write) remains the per-session phrase inside body copy. The five strands are described in **DODO's own words**; the MCT tradition is named **once**, as the lineage of the L/C content — never as the source of each line, never "we teach MCT."

**Bridges (use only where a section needs them):** Poetics also feeds Composition (writing beautifully); Grammar also serves Literacy (four-level analysis of a classic's sentences). Assign each strand its primary branch above.

### The ELA level ladder (replaces the "9 levels / Poodle" framing for ELA)

DODO ELA is a **seven-level** ladder grounded in the gifted-ELA tradition:
**L1 Island → L2 Town → L3 Voyage → L4 Classic Literature → L5 Lens I → L6 Lens II → L7 Lens III** (L7 = college-tier academic English).

- **Poodle / preliminary is not part of ELA.** **Little DODO** — a separate program, independent of this tradition — occupies the early-starter slot. Keep Little DODO out of ELA's methodology narrative. *(Little DODO's own positioning is a later, separate work item.)*
