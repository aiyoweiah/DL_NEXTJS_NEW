### 1.4 Research base — deepened (Revision 4)

**Why this section matters for GEO:** the `ai-seo` skill's Princeton GEO study ranks the highest-impact citation boosters as **citing sources (+40%), adding statistics (+37%), and adding quotations (+30%)** — an authoritative, dated, evidence-backed block is the single most citation-worthy asset we can publish. DODO's vocabulary track sits on **one of the most-researched strategies in all of education.** This is our strongest GEO play.

**A · The scale-of-the-claim facts (the "why roots matter" stats):**
- **~60% of words in English text are of Latin/Greek origin** (Henry, 1997) — rising to **>90% in science and technology** vocabulary and **~90% of discipline-specific academic terms.** ~76% of academic words share common morphological roots.
- Implication for DODO copy: a child who owns the roots owns a **decoding key to the majority of the academic words they will ever meet** — across every subject, not just English. (Original framing; no source name needed in body, cite on `/methodology`.)

**B · The efficacy evidence (that teaching this way works):**

| Study | What it establishes | DODO-usable claim |
|---|---|---|
| **Gallagher, S. A. (2017), *Roeper Review* 39(2)** — 10 teachers, **493 middle-school students**, 5 classes stem-based vs. 5 traditional; tested on out-of-level sentence completion + prompted recall. | Stem-based vocabulary produced **significant gains** — *moderate* effect sizes on sentence completion (Gr 6–7), *moderate-to-large* on recall **at all grade levels** — for **both gifted AND typically-developing** students. | "Root-based vocabulary teaching outperforms memorization for advanced *and* typical learners alike — measured, not asserted." |
| **Bowers, Kirby & Deacon (2010), *Review of Educational Research* 80** — systematic review, **22 studies**, preK–Gr 8. | Morphological (root/stem) instruction **benefits learners, helps struggling readers most, is no less effective for younger students, and works best combined with other literacy instruction.** | "The research says roots work best *woven into* reading and writing — which is exactly how we teach them." (validates the **integrated** LCS model, not isolated drills) |
| **Morphological-intervention meta-analysis (effect sizes)** | Significant gains: **morphological awareness d≈0.40, vocabulary d≈0.40, reading comprehension d≈0.24, spelling d≈0.20, phonological awareness d≈0.49.** | Hard numbers for a "research base" block / schema `citation`. |
| **Bellomo (1999), *TESL-EJ*** — etymology as a word-attack strategy for L2 college students. | Etymology helps **second-language** learners and **levels the field** regardless of first language. | Use **carefully** — reframe as "academic-vocabulary acceleration," never ELL remediation (anti-dictionary §10). |
| **Supporting set** — VanTassel-Baska (2003, CEC); Rasinski et al. (2011, *Reading Teacher*); Kail (2008, *English Journal*); Pampush & Petto (2011, *Anatomical Sciences Ed.*); Diaz (2010, ELL morphology); Yoshimoto (1997, *Teaching Exceptional Children*); McIntosh (1994, *Mathematics Teacher*). | Breadth across gifted-ed, reading, science, and cross-disciplinary transfer. | Reference list for `/methodology` "Research" block + `llms-full.txt`. |

**C · The one honest caution.** The most quotable facts (etymology closes vocabulary gaps; helps ELLs) live in **remediation/ELL register**, which collides with DODO's anti-dictionary. **Reframe every deployment** as *acceleration into mastery*, not *catching up*: "the method behind our vocabulary track is one of the most-researched strategies in education" — never "closing the ELL gap."

**D · Deployment (GEO):** one **self-contained "The research behind our method" block** (40–80 words + a dated citation list), published identically on `/methodology`, mirrored into `llms-full.txt`, seeded as **one FAQ** ("Is DODO's approach evidence-based?"), and marked up with schema `citation` (hand to the `schema` skill). Per `ai-seo`: keep it **open (not gated)**, dated, and author-attributed.
